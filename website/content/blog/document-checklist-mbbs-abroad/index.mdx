One of the most common questions we get isn't about countries or colleges — it's "what do I actually need to have ready?" Here's the honest answer: five documents cover most of it.

## The five documents

**10th marksheet.** Your first formal academic record, and typically the first thing verified.

**12th marksheet.** Confirms your science stream and final scores — the core academic document for a medical programme application.

**Passport.** Needed for visa processing later, but colleges generally want to see it at application stage too, even before travel is imminent. If you don't have one yet, this is worth starting early — passport processing timelines are outside anyone's control but your own.

**A government ID (Aadhaar or equivalent).** Basic identity verification, separate from your passport.

**NEET scorecard.** Ties your application to your qualifying NEET result.

## Format matters more than people expect

A detail that trips people up: marksheets and your NEET scorecard are typically expected as **PDF only**, while your passport and ID can usually be **PDF or a clear image (JPG/PNG)**. Each file is generally capped around **5MB** — a phone-camera photo at full resolution can easily exceed that, so a quick compress or a proper scan is worth doing upfront rather than hitting an upload error later.

## You don't need everything on day one

This is worth repeating: you can start exploring countries and colleges before a single document is ready. Our own application flow treats the country and college selection as separate steps from document upload specifically so you're not blocked from browsing while you gather paperwork. Uploading documents can happen whenever you're ready — it doesn't have to be the first thing you do.

## A simple way to prepare

Scan (don't just photograph) your marksheets if you can — flatter, more consistent, and easier to keep under the size limit. Keep a single folder with all five files named clearly. When you're ready, the [application flow](/apply) walks you through exactly where each one goes.
