If you're considering MBBS abroad, NEET is usually the first thing that comes up — and for good reason. It's not optional. But it's also only one part of a three-piece eligibility picture that's worth understanding before you start picking countries and colleges.

## 1. A qualifying NEET score

To study MBBS abroad and later register to practise in India, you need a qualifying score in the National Eligibility cum Entrance Test (NEET-UG). This applies regardless of which country you study in — it's an Indian regulatory requirement, not something set by the foreign college.

Cutoffs vary by year and category, so we won't quote a fixed number here — check the current year's official NEET qualifying criteria directly rather than relying on a figure that may already be outdated by the time you read it.

## 2. The college must be NMC-recognised

This is the part that gets overlooked. Clearing NEET tells you you're *eligible to consider* studying abroad — it doesn't tell you whether a specific college's degree will actually be recognised when you come back. That's determined separately by the National Medical Commission (NMC).

Before shortlisting any college, it's worth confirming its current recognition status directly, since approvals are reviewed periodically and can change. This is exactly why we screen every college in our own directory for recognition status before listing it — but "on our list" should be a starting point for your own verification, not a substitute for it.

## 3. FMGE, after you graduate

Finishing your MBBS abroad isn't the finish line. To practise medicine in India, graduates of foreign medical colleges need to clear the Foreign Medical Graduate Examination (FMGE) — a licensing exam, not a formality. It's worth factoring into your planning from day one, not treating as an afterthought in your final year.

## Putting it together

In short: NEET gets you in the door, NMC recognition determines whether the degree counts, and FMGE is what stands between graduation and practising. All three matter — treating NEET as the only checkbox is the most common mistake we see.
